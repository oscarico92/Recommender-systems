ScoutAI

Player recommendations for football club recruitment. Recommender Systems, ING5 ECE Paris, Group 6.
Code: github.com/oscarico92/Recommender-systems
Oscar Schwartz (Project Lead) · Simon Gallais (Data Engineer) · Gautier Deplanque (Lead ML Engineer) · Mathis Leitao (ML Engineer, Ops) · Hugo Bassaget (Systems Engineer)

1. Problem and product

Most clubs outside the elite have no data department. Their scouts follow the same well known names, and good players in smaller leagues are never looked at. Existing tools filter players by position, age or value, but they do not learn what a given club actually buys. ScoutAI recommends players to a club: the club is the user, the player is the item, a recruitment is an interaction. It learns each club's recruitment habits from 26,000 real recruitments, recommends players never seen by the graph (cold start), respects the club's budget and favours players whose value goes up. A scout can shortlist or dismiss a player in the app, and the list adapts at once.

2. Data pipeline

Source. Open Transfermarkt dataset (dcaribou/transfermarkt-datasets, CC0, snapshot of 6 July 2026): transfers, players, clubs, 650,000 market valuations and 1.9 million appearances. src/download_data.py downloads the tables, src/clean_transfers.py turns transfers into recruitments, with each rule unit tested and the row count after each step reported:

A manual check confirms the rules (Manchester United's 2023 signings: Mount, Onana, Højlund bought, Evans free, Sabitzer, Weghorst, Amrabat loans), and running on the real data revealed false positives ("B. Leverkusen", "Willem II" taken for reserve teams), fixed with a test. Recent loans cannot be confirmed because the return has not happened yet: they are flagged possible_loan. A weight of 0.5 for loans was tested on validation and gave no gain, so loans count as full recruitments.

Player features (src/features.py): 48 features computed at a cutoff date: age, height, position, nationality, market value, peak value and 12-month trend, minutes, goals, assists and cards per 90, European games, share of minutes in the 10 leagues, past transfers. Only rows dated strictly before the cutoff are used; snapshot fields describing today (current club, value, contract) are never used. A test adds future rows and checks that the features do not change. New interactions from the app (POST /log_interaction: viewed, shortlisted, dismissed, signed) are stored in SQLite.

3. Model

LightGCN (He et al., SIGIR 2020), PyTorch Geometric implementation, on the bipartite club / player graph of the last 10 years before the cutoff, BPR loss with uniform negative sampling. Grid search on the validation season with early stopping: 2 layers, dimension 64, 35 epochs.

Cold-start encoder. About 60% of the catalogue has no recruitment in the graph, and 35% of the players signed in the test season are among them. An MLP maps the 48 features to the LightGCN embedding space, trained by distillation on the players that are in the graph (in the spirit of DropoutNet). It ranks a separate "new talents" list: mixing them into the main top 10 lowered validation NDCG@10, so it is off by default.

Re-ranking of the 50 best candidates: players above the club's budget (90th percentile of the values it signed in 3 years) go last, then the score is combined with the 12-month value trend (resale) and penalties for age and for frequent movers. The weights are chosen on the validation season over 3 seeds: the strongest age weight whose NDCG@10 stays within one standard deviation of the best (resale 1, age 2, mobility 0).

Explanations: past signings of the club in the same position, ordered by embedding similarity (95% same position); for new talents a profile rule. Feedback: a shortlist or dismissal hides the player and moves the club vector toward or away from him (Rocchio update), at once, without retraining.

4. Evaluation protocol

  • Temporal split: train before 1 July 2024, tuning on season 2024/25, test on season 2025/26. The test model is retrained on everything before 1 July 2025 with the tuned settings; the test season is never used for training, features, graph or tuning (audit in docs/week3_evidence.md).
  • Candidates: 17,628 active players at the cutoff (valued in the previous year, 36 or younger), minus the club's past signings and current squad. Targets: 2,993 recruitments, 90% of them in the catalogue.
  • Metrics: NDCG@10 and Recall@20 against random, most popular (all history and last 2 seasons) and highest market value; cold-start NDCG@10; share of the top 10 within budget; value change of the top 10 in the following year; mean age; coverage. Every model is trained with 3 seeds (mean ± std).

What the results show. (1) Sanity floor: LightGCN reaches 5 times the NDCG@10 of both most popular rankings, with separated standard deviations, and recommends 70 times more distinct players (coverage 5.7% against 0.08%): it does not just push popular names. (2) Budget: the re-ranking keeps 98% of the top 10 affordable against 76% without it. (3) Age bias corrected: LightGCN alone favours experienced players who moved many times (29.1 years, value -0.21 the next year); the tuned re-ranking brings the top 10 to 25.6 years and a positive value change, while NDCG@10 goes from 0.0098 to 0.0162, a gap larger than the seed noise. (4) Cold start: the encoder finds new talents 5 times better than "highest market value" and 50 times better than random. Absolute values are low because each club signs about 10 players among 17,628 candidates.

Honest limits

  • Run-to-run instability: two trainings with different seeds share only 35% of a club's top 10, although the averaged metrics are stable (± 0.0024). The demo uses the committed model and is not retrained before it.
  • Feedback adapts, it does not learn: on the validation season, using a club's first 2 signings as a shortlist leaves NDCG@10 on its later signings unchanged (0.0094 with and without the update); a random shortlist lowers it (0.0089). Retraining with feedback is within seed noise. We claim that the list adapts to the scout, not that it becomes more accurate.
  • Data: loans are inferred; the league of a club is its current one; "new talents" also contains established players who have not moved in 10 years; the lists stay older than real signings (25.6 against 24.5) because the re-ranking only reorders 50 candidates.

6. System and reproducibility

The Streamlit interface shows player cards with photo, club crest, value trend and a scouting report (radar of the player's statistics against his position, value curve), a new talents tab, and a shortlist where 2 or 3 players can be compared. The trained model is exported to models/serving/ and versioned, so the app runs with pip install -r requirements.txt (no PyTorch, no data download): install in about 30 seconds on a clean machine, answers in 20 to 40 ms. Retraining is documented step by step in the README. 62 automated tests cover the cleaning rules, feature leakage, metrics, re-ranking, feedback, API and the Streamlit page. Every change went through a reviewed pull request.
